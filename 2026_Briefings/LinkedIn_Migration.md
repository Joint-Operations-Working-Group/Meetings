Chile is an incredible country to explore, stretching across vastly different climates from the driest desert in the world down to glacial fjords. Planning a trip there involves a few specific logistics, particularly around entry paperwork, flights, and managing local taxes.

Here is a practical breakdown of the key essentials for a trip from Europe:

### Visas & Entry

* **90-Day Entry:** European passport holders (including EU, UK, and Swiss citizens) do not need to apply for a visa in advance for tourist stays under 90 days.
* **The PDI Card:** When passing through immigration, you will be handed a small receipt-style slip called the *Tarjeta de Turismo* (Tourist Card) issued by the PDI (Investigations Police). Keep this safely tucked inside your passport. It is required to exit the country and serves a financial purpose at hotels.

### Flight Hubs & Routes

* **The Gateway:** All long-haul international flights arrive at **Arturo Merino Benítez International Airport (SCL)** in Santiago.
* **Direct Routes:** Direct flights from Europe operate primarily out of Madrid (LATAM and Iberia), Paris (Air France), and London Heathrow (British Airways).
* **Connecting Options:** If you don't mind a stopover, routing through Latin American hubs like São Paulo (GRU) or Bogotá (BOG) often provides competitive fares.

### Electricity & Plugs

* **Plug Styles:** Chile uses **Type C** and **Type L** plugs. Type C is the standard European two-pin plug, meaning low-voltage devices like phone chargers often plug straight into the wall without an adapter.
* **Three-Pin Grounded Plugs:** For grounded devices requiring three prongs, Chile uses Type L, where the third grounding pin sits directly in line between the other two.
* **Voltage:** The grid runs on 220V at 50Hz, perfectly compatible with European appliances.

### Taxes & Accommodation Savings

* **The 19% IVA Rule:** Chile has a standard Value Added Tax (IVA) of 19%. However, international tourists can legally bypass this tax on accommodation.
* **How to Qualify:** To get the 19% tax exemption on your hotel room, you must pay in US dollars (either in cash or using an international credit card) and present your foreign passport along with that PDI Tourist Card received at immigration. Always check your booking details to ensure the hotel hasn't pre-packaged the tax into a non-refundable rate.
